Half a year ago, I realized I had gotten way too comfortable.

I was good at my job. Maybe that was exactly the problem. 

Work had become familiar and routine. I knew what needed to be done, I knew how to do it, and honestly, I could probably do some of it with my eyes closed. It wasn't necessarily a bad thing, but after a while, I started feeling like I wasn't learning anymore, not being challenged, just... there.

Nothing was dramatically wrong. I wasn’t struggling. I didn’t hate what I did. I wasn’t just learning as much. I wasn’t wondering how things worked because, most of the time, I already knew. I felt stagnant.

There was this itch to completely rehaul my life. I don't even fully know how to explain it, but I didn't like the place I was in at that time. I'd look at other people and think about how they seemed to know things I didn't, things I also wanted to understand, and I just felt stuck in design.

The funny thing is, wanting change and actually doing something about it are two very different things.

Leaving the company I had been with for four years scared me. Four years is a long time. You get used to the work, the people (shoutout to the design team), the routine, even the little things you complain about every day. There was comfort in knowing what tomorrow would look like.

So of course my brain went through every possible scenario, and somehow they all led to quitting.

What if the next job was worse? What if I was just frustrated and making an impulsive decision? What if I wasn't actually as good as I thought I was? What if I left something stable and ended up regretting it?

I had no idea.

I just knew I wanted to change something (apparently, cutting my hair and giving myself bangs was not enough). I didn't want to stay somewhere simply because I was scared of what would happen if I left.

So I handed in my resignation last December and signed an offer from a new company right after.

Was I confident about it? Nope.

I was scared, frustrated, shocked by my own decision, and very, very uncertain, but somehow there was also peace. A lightness, like my life was finally in motion again.

I did it anyway.

## So... what do I actually do now?

Good question. 😂

These days, I wake up wondering what version of myself work is going to need.

Some days I'm a UI designer, some days a UX engineer, some days a full-stack dev. Then suddenly I'm handling CI/CD, managing databases, or doing things I barely had experience with before. And most days, I'm juggling a couple of projects at the same time.

At this point, I just open my laptop and find out who I am for the day. 😂

The projects themselves have been all over the place too. Stocks and finance, mental health, environmental projects, CMS, and other industries I probably wouldn't have imagined myself working in before. One minute I'm learning a completely new field, and the next I'm expected to understand enough of it to actually answer clients' questions and do whatever it is that they want for their projects.

Then there are the random MS Teams group chats I suddenly get added to with almost zero context. Meetings I enter thinking I'm just there to listen or make the site pretty, only to realize halfway through that I apparently have responsibilities too.

Or my personal favorite: sitting in a room full of people discussing a project I haven't even been properly briefed on yet, then receiving the project brief email mid-meeting.

Like... Yeah. So that's what we're talking about. Got it. 😂

And then there are the meetings where I look around and suddenly realize I'm the only woman in the room. Kinda Barbie in a world of Kens, right? Purr. Slay. Kinda Chic.

Somewhere in between all of that, I've also gotten the chance to work with international companies and meet people I never imagined I'd cross paths with.

Sometimes I think about what my work life looked like just half a year ago compared to now, and it feels a little insane.

In a good way. Mostly. 😂

## "I'm scared to touch this."

The few people I confide in know how often they hear this from me.

Especially when a database is involved.

Because hear me out, there is a very specific kind of fear that comes with looking at a production database and knowing one wrong move could make your day significantly more interesting. I could be demoted to just a client in a snap.

My first reaction to some tasks is genuinely, I do not want to be the person who breaks this.

Sometimes I stare at something for a while before I even start. Sometimes I ask a lot of questions, sometimes I don't ask at all and just wing it. Sometimes I read the same documentation five times. Sometimes I double check, triple check, then check one more time because why not.

And yes, I keep backups. I may be brave, but I am not stupid haha.

Eventually though, I do the thing. And most of the time, it turns out fine... I think.

Then I get another task I've never done before, and yep, there we go again.

## Maybe this is just how I grow

I used to think growth meant reaching a point where I'd feel confident and know exactly what I'm doing, like I'd suddenly become an experienced developer who can look at any problem and say, "I know how to fix this."

Instead, the more I learn, the more I realize how much I don't know. And weirdly, I think I'm becoming more okay with that.

Maybe growth for me isn't about knowing everything before I say yes to something. Maybe it's getting thrown into something unfamiliar and thinking, Okay... how the f\*\* do I do this? Then figuring it out.

Maybe growth is realizing that I'm not as smart as I think I am, and creating enough space for new learning to come in.

Maybe it's asking questions even when part of me feels like I should already know the answer. Maybe it's joining a meeting completely unbriefed and somehow leaving with a new project. Maybe it's being scared to touch the database and touching it anyway (with backups, obviously).

I'm still scared sometimes. I still get tasks that make me question whether I'm ready for them. There is still so much I don't know.

But I'm slowly realizing I don't have to feel ready first.

I left my old job scared. I started this one scared. I've taken on projects scared. I've touched databases scared, super scared. 😂 I'll probably always be scared.

And somehow, every time I manage to figure out something I once thought I couldn't do, the next scary thing starts to feel just a little less scary.

So maybe this is just my way of growing.

Jump first. Panic a little or a lot, whatever. Ask questions and be teachable. Figure it out along the way.

And looking at how much my life has changed in just half a year, I think I'm actually okay with growing like this.
